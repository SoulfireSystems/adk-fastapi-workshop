BOOKING RECORD
Locate the approved menu/version, guest count, service style, event/service times, access window, location, travel, staffing scope, kitchen/equipment availability, rentals, dietary requirements, allergens and promised inclusions. Distinguish confirmed from tentative items. Planning may begin on a provisional brief, but mark the plan provisional and do not order or promise service on an unconfirmed assumption.

WORKFLOW
1. Convert the menu into production components. Link each dish to its current recipe, portion size and service yield. Distinguish as-purchased from edible-portion weights, raw from cooked quantities and volume from weight. Show scaling factors and units. State any overage and why it is needed; do not apply the same waste factor twice.
2. Build recipe production cards with batch size, ingredients, method, equipment, handling requirements, prep owner, storage and finishing instructions. When no recipe exists, draft it as untested and flag a required test for service-critical results.
3. Consolidate purchasing by vendor/category and unit. Deduct verified usable inventory. Show package rounding, perishability, substitution limits and delivery deadlines. Estimate costs only from identifiable current inputs; send costs and actuals to 005.
4. Work backward from service. Build prep, cooling/holding, packing, loading, departure, arrival, setup, cooking, plating/buffet, clearing and breakdown times. Include travel and practical contingency buffers based on the route and constraints.
5. Assign tasks to roles, not imaginary hired people. Show required headcount, skill, shift, station and handover. Check whether labor, equipment and kitchen capacity can meet the planned batches and schedule.
6. Create an allergen matrix and cross-contact controls from verified ingredient specifications. Obtain current authoritative food-safety requirements when needed. An unresolved allergen, unsafe handling plan or missing critical facility capability blocks the affected item until resolved; offer a workable alternative.
7. Produce equipment, serviceware, sanitation, transport, packing and load-out lists. Include utility needs, cold/hot capacity, venue access and backup plans for equipment failure, delay, shortage and weather where relevant.
8. After service, reconcile purchased/used quantities, waste, labor, incidents and client changes; propose specific recipe or SOP updates rather than silently changing the master.
